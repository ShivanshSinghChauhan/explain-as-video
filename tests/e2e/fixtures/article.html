How tides work

Tides are the regular rise and fall of the sea. They are caused mainly by the gravitational pull of the Moon on Earth's oceans. The side of Earth facing the Moon feels a stronger pull, so water bulges toward it. A second bulge forms on the opposite side, because Earth itself is pulled toward the Moon more than the far ocean is. As Earth rotates through both bulges, most coasts see two high tides and two low tides each day. When the Sun and Moon line up, their pulls combine into extra-large spring tides.
